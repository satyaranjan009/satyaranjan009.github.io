About

I am a Marie Curie Doctoral Fellow at the Fritz Haber Research Centre for Molecular Dynamics, Hebrew University of Jerusalem, working under Prof. Noam Agmon. My research lies at the intersection of computational biophysics, quantum chemistry, and machine learning — with a particular focus on proton and ion transport mechanisms in hydrated biological macromolecules.

Using classical Molecular Dynamics and hybrid QM/MM simulations, I investigate proton transfer along protein surfaces and collagen-like peptides, aiming to elucidate the biophysical basis of processes fundamental to cell bioenergetics, enzyme catalysis, and disease mechanisms.

Research

My work spans three research environments — combining classical MD, QM/MM, and machine learning to study proton transport, protein hydration, and drug discovery.

2020 – Present · Marie Curie Fellow · Hebrew University of Jerusalem

Proton Transport in Biological Systems

Doctoral Advisor: Prof. Noam Agmon — Fritz Haber Research Centre for Molecular Dynamics

  • Develop in-silico models of collagen-like triple helices
  • Study hydration layer structure and solvation water dynamics
  • Explicit-water simulations to analyze hydration layer's electrostatic surface potential (ESP)
  • Investigate whether ions and protons migrate along the protein surface
  • Introduce proton models to test surface proton mobility mechanisms

Carboxylate-Grafted Collagen & Proton Transfer

Supervisors: Prof. Noam Agmon & Prof. Ana-Nicoleta Bondar

  • Developing biocompatible, carboxylate-enriched collagen peptides as proton-conducting "wires"
  • Modify collagen with aspartate and glutamate residues
  • Identify water wires and proton-hopping pathways
  • Examine proton mobility between carboxyl groups
