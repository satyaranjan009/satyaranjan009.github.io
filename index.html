About

I am a Marie Curie Doctoral Fellow at the Fritz Haber Research Centre for Molecular Dynamics, Hebrew University of Jerusalem, working under Prof. Noam Agmon. My research lies at the intersection of computational biophysics, quantum chemistry, and machine learning — with a particular focus on proton and ion transport mechanisms in hydrated biological macromolecules.

Using classical Molecular Dynamics and hybrid QM/MM simulations, I investigate proton transfer along protein surfaces and collagen-like peptides, aiming to elucidate the biophysical basis of cell bioenergetics, enzyme catalysis, and disease mechanisms.

Research

My research spans computational biophysics, molecular dynamics, quantum chemistry, and machine learning applied to biological and chemical systems.

Molecular Dynamics Simulation

Atomistic and coarse-grained MD simulations to study conformational dynamics, thermodynamic properties, and structural behaviour of biomolecular systems.

Collagen Triple Helix

Structural and mechanical investigation of the collagen triple-helix motif, probing hydrogen-bond networks and fibril stability through MD simulations.

Surface Mobility

Investigating the diffusion and mobility of molecules on biological surfaces, with implications for membrane dynamics and cell signalling.

QM/MM Simulation

Hybrid quantum mechanics / molecular mechanics (QM/MM) approaches for modelling enzyme active sites, chemical reactions, and electronic effects in biomolecules.

Biophysical Modelling

Advanced computational modelling of biophysical processes including protein folding, aggregation, and interactions with small molecules.

Drug Target Docking

Molecular docking and free-energy perturbation calculations to predict drug–receptor binding modes and guide rational drug design.

Density Functional Theory (DFT)

Electronic-structure calculations using DFT to study reaction mechanisms, charge distributions, and spectroscopic properties of molecular systems.

Machine Learning

Applying neural networks and deep-learning frameworks to accelerate force-field development, property prediction, and analysis of large biomolecular simulation data.
